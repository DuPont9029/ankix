Renal Physiology

Lecture 7 · Filtration, reabsorption and urine concentration

Human Physiology · Medicine and Surgery

Lecture 7 — 1/5

Glomerular filtration

  • Filtrate forms in the glomerulus and enters Bowman's capsule.
  • A normal glomerular filtration rate (GFR) is about 125 mL/min (≈ 180 L/day).
  • It depends on Starling forces: capillary hydrostatic pressure against oncotic pressure and capsular pressure.
  • Creatinine clearance is used clinically to estimate GFR.
Lecture 7 — 2/5

Tubular reabsorption

  • The proximal convoluted tubule reabsorbs about 65% of filtered sodium and water, plus glucose and amino acids.
  • The thick ascending limb reabsorbs NaCl through the Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ cotransporter and is impermeable to water.
  • In the distal tubule and collecting duct, aldosterone increases Na⁺ reabsorption and K⁺ secretion.
Lecture 7 — 3/5

Concentrating the urine

  • The loop of Henle builds an osmotic gradient in the medulla through countercurrent multiplication.
  • The descending limb is permeable to water; the ascending limb is not.
  • Urea recycling adds to the osmolarity of the inner medulla.
  • ADH inserts aquaporin-2 into the collecting duct membrane so water can be reabsorbed.
Lecture 7 — 4/5

Control: the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system

  • Renin is released by juxtaglomerular cells of the afferent arteriole when perfusion pressure falls.
  • Angiotensin II constricts arterioles and stimulates aldosterone and ADH release.
  • Tubuloglomerular feedback (macula densa) matches GFR to the NaCl load in the distal tubule.
